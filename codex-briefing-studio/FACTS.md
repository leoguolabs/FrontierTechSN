# Research notes

The film separates assisted performance, unaided performance, delayed retention,
and transfer. These are related outcomes, not interchangeable measurements.

## Turkey mathematics experiment

**Source:** Bastani et al., *Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning*,
PNAS, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122);
[author-hosted paper](https://hamsabastani.github.io/education_llm.pdf).

The field experiment involved nearly 1,000 high-school mathematics students in
Turkey. Compared with the control condition, unrestricted GPT Base improved
practice grades by 48%, but reduced subsequent unaided exam grades by 17%.
Teacher-supplied hints in GPT Tutor largely removed that penalty; the study did
not establish a statistically significant positive exam effect for that tutor.

**Graphics:** 148 and 83 are illustrative indices computed from the reported
relative effects, setting the relevant control outcome to 100. They are not
observed scores, percentage-point effects, or the same student's before-and-after
grades. The two tests have separate control outcomes.

**Boundary:** A short-term mathematics experiment does not demonstrate permanent
cognitive damage or that all AI tutoring is ineffective.

## College physics experiment

**Source:** Kestin, Miller, Klales, Milbourne, and Ponti (2025),
[*AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6).

A randomized crossover study compared a designed AI tutor with active classroom
lessons in an undergraduate physics course. The tutor condition had higher
immediate post-test scores. This is a different intervention, population, and
assessment from the Turkey study. The film does not put both experiments on one
numerical axis or infer months-long retention.

The classroom drawings and formula cards are explanatory illustrations, not
photographs of participants or reproductions of the experimental test items.

## Learning, practice, and guidance

**Primary experiment:** Sweller and Cooper (1985),
[*The Use of Worked Examples as a Substitute for Problem Solving in Learning Algebra*](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s1532690xci0201_3).
Worked examples helped novices solve subsequent structurally similar problems
more quickly and with fewer errors. This does not establish unrestricted transfer.

**Research synthesis by the researchers' laboratory:**
[UCLA Bjork Learning and Forgetting Lab](https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/research/).
Performance during practice can be an unreliable guide to durable learning.
Retrieval, spacing, and the conditions of practice matter.

**Guidance as expertise grows:**
[NSW Centre for Education Statistics and Evaluation](https://education.nsw.gov.au/about-us/education-data-and-research/cese/publications/literature-reviews/cognitive-load-theory).

The film's final three-step routine is an editorial application of these ideas:
appropriate help, an unaided explanation followed by correction, and a later
attempt on a changed problem. It is not a tested universal protocol, and the
film explicitly allows a novice to begin with a worked example.

All claims above were checked against the linked sources during production.
No claims about model supremacy, permanent harm, or universal learning effects
are made.
